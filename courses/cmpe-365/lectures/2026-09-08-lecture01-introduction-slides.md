## What is an algorithm?

- **Informally:** "an ordered sequence of instructions that is guaranteed to solve a specific
  problem."
- **Formally:** "a well-ordered collection of unambiguous and effective computable operations
  that, when executed, produces a result and **halts in a finite amount of time**."

Computer science is framed as the study of algorithms — their formal/mathematical properties,
hardware realizations, linguistic realizations, and applications. The stack given:
algorithmic foundation → hardware → virtual machine → software world → applications → ethics.

Why formal algorithms matter: if you can specify an algorithm, you can **automate** the solution —
build a machine or write a program to carry out the steps.

## Learning expectations

- Know how to **analyze** the efficiency of an algorithm.
- Know the major **algorithmic paradigms**.
- Know how to **design** a correct and efficient algorithm.

## What the instructor says you actually get

1. **A head start on technical interviews** — writing code and explaining the design on the spot,
   showing your thought process on novel problems, online assessments and on-site interviews.
2. **Training to solve hard and novel problems** — active learning, self-reflection, pushing your
   limit and learning from setbacks.

## How to succeed (instructor's list)

Come to lectures · review each lecture and **recreate the algorithms** · self-reflect after each
chapter and test · get help · send feedback.

**Advice from last year's students:** attend lectures regularly · work practice problems every week
· use the resources · start early and stay consistent · engage and collaborate · ask for help ·
mindset and strategy · prepare effectively for tests · take care of yourself.
