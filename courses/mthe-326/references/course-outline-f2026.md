## Expanded course description

Complex analysis is the calculus of functions of one complex variable. The subject begins with a
modest-looking change — allowing the variable to be complex rather than real — and produces a
theory strikingly more rigid and more powerful than real calculus: a function differentiable *once*
on an open set is automatically differentiable infinitely often and equal to its own Taylor series;
the values of such a function on a closed contour determine it everywhere inside; and integrals
difficult or impossible by real methods reduce to a finite sum of residues.

Topics, in the order they are treated:

1. **Complex numbers** — algebra, geometry, polar form, roots, and the topology of the complex plane.
2. **Analytic functions** — limits, differentiability, the Cauchy–Riemann equations, harmonic functions.
3. **Elementary functions** — the complex exponential, trigonometric and hyperbolic functions, logarithms, powers, and branches.
4. **Complex integration** — contours and contour integrals, Cauchy's theorem, Cauchy's integral formula.
5. **Power and Laurent series** — zeros and singularities.
6. **Residue theory** — calculating real and complex integrals using residues.

## Learning outcomes

All five are **essential requirements**: each is core content that must be demonstrated to pass,
with or without accommodations. The standards cannot be waived, though *how* they are demonstrated
can be adapted through an approved accommodation.

- **LO 1** — Work fluently with complex numbers and the elementary complex functions (exponential,
  logarithmic, trigonometric, power), identifying their branches and domains of analyticity.
- **LO 2** — Determine whether a function is analytic or harmonic using the Cauchy–Riemann
  equations, and construct harmonic conjugates.
- **LO 3** — Evaluate contour integrals, both directly from the definition and via Cauchy's theorem
  and Cauchy's integral formula.
- **LO 4** — Expand functions in power and Laurent series, and use these to locate and classify
  zeros and singularities.
- **LO 5** — Calculate residues and apply the residue theorem to evaluate contour integrals and
  definite real integrals.

## GenAI policy

**Position: permitted while studying, not permitted on any assessment.**

- **Permitted** — using GenAI and any other technology while learning on your own time: suggested
  exercises, posted problem sets, reading, asking for a definition to be explained, working through
  an example, checking an algebraic step. No disclosure or citation required, because none of it is
  submitted for grades.
- **Not permitted** — **no aids of any kind** during quizzes, midterms, or the final: no GenAI, no
  calculators, no notes, no devices. These account for **100% of the course grade**. Using a
  prohibited aid, or submitting AI-generated content as your own, is a departure from academic
  integrity.

The instructor adds an unusually direct caution, worth reading in full as written:

> "I discourage you from relying on generative AI in this course, even where it is permitted. […]
> These tools are unreliable on exactly the material we are covering. They routinely produce
> confident, fluent, wrong complex analysis — mishandled branch cuts, invented residues, contours
> that do not close, theorems applied where their hypotheses fail. If you cannot yet tell a correct
> argument from a plausible-sounding one, you cannot tell when the tool has misled you, and that is
> precisely the skill this course is meant to build. […] Mathematical understanding is built by
> struggling with a problem you cannot immediately solve, and every time you hand that struggle to
> a machine you skip the part where the learning actually happens."

> 🔑 **This constrains how this repo should be used for MTHE 326.** Explanations and worked examples
> for *studying* are within the stated policy; anything resembling an answer key for an assessment
> is not, and no assessment in this course is take-home anyway. The instructor's substantive point
> — that fluent-but-wrong complex analysis is hard to detect until you can do it yourself — is a
> good reason to lean on `quiz-me` here and to verify every worked contour integral by hand.

promptly; email answered within two business days.

they are almost always underused" — come with a specific question, or with none and work problems
in the room. Beyond that: the weekly tutorial, the Mathematics and Statistics Help Centre
