# ENPH 334 — Lab Book Marking Scheme and Common Debugging First Steps

> The **rubric the lab notebook is marked against** (7% of the course grade, marked in week 11,
> submitted for feedback only in week 4), plus a troubleshooting checklist. Applies to every lab in
> this folder. Source: `00-lab-book-marking-and-debugging.pdf`, **Version 2, dated 17/09/19**.
>
> 📝 That date is **2017-09-19** — this document predates the current instructor and has been
> reissued unchanged. It is still the operative rubric (the [syllabus](../references/syllabus-f2026.md)
> asks for the same things), but where the two differ, the syllabus is newer.

## TLDR

**Each section is marked out of 5, and the notebook is prepared ahead of time, not written up
after.** Six sections: general organization, graphs/tables, circuit diagrams, equipment used,
commentary/debugging, and an end-of-session record. The single most load-bearing requirement is
that **circuit diagrams with component values and pin numbers are drawn *before* you wire
anything** — and that you **log what you tried while debugging**, not just what finally worked.

## Common debugging first steps

Work down this list before asking a TA:

- **Fuse blown on protoboard?**
- **Circuit constructed correctly?**
  - Nodes connected correctly?
  - Right components?
  - Individual components working correctly?
  - Power on?
- **Can you measure another quantity in your circuit** to verify that the circuit is working
  correctly?
- **Measurement equipment set up correctly?**
  - Voltage or timescales correct?
  - Reading the right channel?
  - **AC vs DC settings** on the oscilloscope or DMM?
- **Do you understand what you are measuring?**
- **Do you have an alternative method or technique for comparison?**
  - Simulations — *how long will it take to create and run a simulation?*
  - Theoretical calculations
  - Alternative instrument measurement

## Lab book marking scheme

> **Each section is marked out of 5. Prepare the lab book ahead of time.**

### General organization
- Referencing the instructions on OnQ
- **File names and locations** for simulations or important documents, and a description of what is
  in each file
- **Dates on all pages**
- **Titles on sections**, even for roughwork and calculations
- **Table of contents** kept up to date
- **Pages numbered**
- **Use pen**, even when graphing

### Graphs and tables
- Titles
- Axis labels
- Drawn neatly
- **Ruler used**
- Short description of the graph/table contents

### Circuit diagrams
- Neatly drawn
- **Proper symbols** used
- Titled and labelled
- **Values of components** in the circuit
- **Sources used** for the circuit — function generator? protoboard? battery? other?
- Where did you get special or irregular components?

### Equipment used during the lab
- Note the **name, make, model and instrument type** used in each lab
- **User manual file names and locations**
- Reference the page where you introduced the instrument, if it is the same every lab

### Commentary, debugging comments, and questions from the lab instructions
- **Neat writing — can someone else read it?**
- Clearly answers the questions
- Includes commentary on troubleshooting equipment
- **Not verbose** — comment on serious, time-consuming debugging efforts:
  - Say **what you tried and why it did not work**
  - **Log steps so you do not repeat yourself** — process of elimination
- Reference figures and tables with labels and page numbers

## At the end of each session

**If you did not finish the lab, record:**
- Why you did not finish
- What was completed
- What is left to do in the lab/project
- How long you worked during the session

**If you did finish the lab, record:**
- That you completed the lab
- How long it took you to complete the lab/work

> 🔑 **Why the end-of-session record matters more than it looks.** Lab attendance is mandatory and
> the whole 39% lab section is zeroed if labs are not completed satisfactorily — so the notebook is
> the written record of what was and was not finished, and of the make-up you arranged. See the
> [syllabus](../references/syllabus-f2026.md) on completion sign-offs.
